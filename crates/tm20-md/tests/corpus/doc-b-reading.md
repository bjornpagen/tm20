# On the tape

A column of 576 dots is a page you hold. The measure is fixed; the height is not. What follows is a short reading on why that column is enough, with a little algebra and a note.[^n1]

## Measure

Body type on this printer is 11 pt Helvetica, rag right, about forty characters to the line. That is a newspaper column, not a book page. The discipline is the same: one idea per sentence, one sentence per breath, and a rule when the subject changes.

The usable width is \(576\) dots at \(203\) dpi. In millimetres that is

\[
\frac{576}{8} = 72
\]

which is the paper. Nothing in the dialect can ask for more.

## Composition

A sheet is a sequence of frames. Rhythm between them is a table, not a taste. Prose after prose takes a slug; a head after prose takes a slug; a rule after prose takes none. The extra is computed from the last rhythm and the next one. That is the whole of `compose::extra`.

> White is adjacency, not a skip you type.

The quote above is the house rule. A blank line in the source is a paragraph break. It is not a request for a bigger skip. If you want a rule, write a rule.

## Notes and math

A citation becomes a number in the same sequence as a link.[^n2] Inline math stays in the line: the Faraday field is \(F=\mathbf{E}+Ic\mathbf{B}\). Display math sits on its own row and is centered if it fits.

\[
\nabla F = \mu_{0} c J
\]

The second band starts somewhere in this essay. That is the point: a reading has to be tall enough to cross the 910-dot payload cap, or it is only a fixture.

## Close

The printer never sees a font. The whole file encodes before the USB device opens. A document this long is still one raster, then a partition. The partition is not the typesetter's problem; the height is.

[^n1]: The tape is 80 mm stock; the printable measure is 72 mm.
[^n2]: Link notes and footnotes share one apparatus, numbered by first appearance.
